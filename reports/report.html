4. What drives the result — displacement and mean reversion

The center strike K is set from the previous close. By 10:00am entry, SPX has usually already moved. Define:

  • d = SPX at 10:00 (export Price at Open) − K — morning displacement
  • m = settlement − SPX at 10:00 — afternoon move after entry
  • final miss = |settlement − K| — alone determines P/L given the credit

If the afternoon undoes the morning (m opposite d), the trade is a mean-reversion bet. If m is near zero regardless of d, it is mainly a bet that SPX stays near the center.

4.1 Where does SPX sit at entry?

57% of trades enter with SPX already outside the 10-point wings, and only 11% start within 2.5 points of the center. Bucket edges use each trade’s own fill credit (so “inside credit” means a win if SPX does not move further). Counts are therefore unequal by design — they reflect how often the strategy is already asking for a pullback.

4.2 Does the afternoon reverse the morning? (central test)

Regress post-entry move m on displacement d. A significantly negative slope means large morning moves tend to reverse after 10:00 (mean reversion). A slope near zero means the afternoon is unrelated to how far SPX already traveled — closer to a “calm afternoon / pin” bet. The dotted line m = −d is full snap-back to the center.

Fitted line: m = 0.14 + -0.10 · d (r = -0.07, t-stat on slope = -1.5, n = 508). The slope is not clearly significant (|t| ≤ 2): treat mean reversion as weak or inconclusive.

4.4 Credit vs displacement (why the 9.65 filter matters)

As |d| grows, the iron butterfly mid credit rises toward the wing width. Correlation between fill credit and |d| is 0.74. The 9.65 max-mid filter therefore acts as an implicit displacement filter: the most extreme morning moves are more likely to be skipped. 5. Strike-grid rounding

The center strike is previous close + $0.01 rounded onto the 5.0-point SPX grid, so the rounding error (K − previous close) cannot exceed ±2.5 points. Median |error| is 1.20 points — an order of magnitude smaller than typical entry displacement (|d|).

Conclusion: Grid rounding shifts the center by at most 2.5 points (median |error| 1.20); up- vs down-rounding avg P/L $35 vs $35 — the 5-point grid does not materially affect results. Up-rounding (251 days) and down-rounding (255 days) have nearly the same average P/L ($35 vs $35) and win rates (25% vs 26%). Correlation of grid error with trade P/L is 0.01.
